Away is a simple, almost bare bones animated adventure movie, that evokes the wonder of adventure but feels too lacking in the excitement that accompanies it.

Slow yet steady, Away puts us immediately in the shoes of a young boy who seems to have survived a plane crash. From there we follow him as he tries make his way back to civilization.

This movie is able to capture a sense of serenity of a solo adventure, a sort of peaceful alienation from your usual surroundings. This is both enhanced and at times hampered by the animation style/quality. Seemingly block and lifeless, yet at times beautiful the lack of consistency and visual depth takes away from the overall experience rather than enhances it. This does not mean that there aren't moments where you wish it could have been you riding through a lake reflecting the sky with birds flying all around you.

All of this is to say that the movie is trying to be profound, to be deep to have some hidden meaning beyond the visuals and the destination. But, again it fails to come across clearly. Is the hollow translucent spirit something from the boy's past? something more psychological? grief? depression? loneliness perhaps? It was a little too open to interpretation in my opinion. Other things do help elicit thoughts of depth like the motorcycle being a direct contrast to the vast apathetic wilderness which the boy is journeying across. The bike feels like a stand in for humanity's drive for adventure and their will to overcome and reach their destination.

In summation, there is a strong base for a good movie here. I believe as a debut it's solid, and I hope to see what this new director has to offer next time.
